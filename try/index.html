A 10-minute exercise

Meet a part of you

This takes about 10 minutes. Find a quiet spot where nobody needs you for a while.

You'll need either a pen and a piece of paper, or a small object you can hold: a stone, a mug, a key. Anything works.

Nothing you write here is saved. At the end, you can email yourself a little note of what you found.

First: what is a part?

You already talk in parts without noticing. Part of me wants to go to the party, part of me wants to stay home.

Most of the time, though, it doesn't feel like a part of us has a feeling. It feels like we are the feeling.

I notice it in myself when someone asks me what I'd like, and my mind goes blank. For a moment, I'm not someone who notices the blank. I am the blank.

In parts work, that's called being blended. A part has stepped so close that you can't see it anymore. You see the world through its eyes.

Now notice the difference

“I'm furious.”

and

“A part of me is furious.”

Read both slowly. The second one leaves a little room. Someone is noticing the fury. That someone is you.

Stepping into that room is called unblending.

It doesn't mean pushing the part away. There are no bad parts. Every part is trying to help you in its own way, even the loud and difficult ones. Unblending just gives you enough space to actually see it.

That's what you'll try now, in four small steps: find it, give it a form, make some space, and notice how you feel toward it.

Find it

Where do you want to start?

Pick something small or medium for this first time. Not the biggest thing in your life.

Just enough so you'll recognise it later.

Find it

Let it be there

Go back into that moment for a few seconds. Where were you? Who was there? What happened just before? Then notice what happens in your body as you remember.

Close your eyes for a moment and bring your attention to the sensation. You don't need to change it. Just let it be there.

Take your time. Come back when you can feel it.

Then answer whatever comes first. Don't think too hard. “I don't know” is an answer too.

One sentence is plenty.
Parts are usually trying to protect us from something.

Give it a form

Let it come out of you

Right now this part lives inside you. Let's give it something it can live in outside of you for a few minutes.

Optional. Something like “the Guard”, “the Hurry”, “Little Boss”.

Make some space

Put it in front of you

Place it on the table or the floor in front of you.

Look at it. That's the part. And you are the one looking at it.

Now move it a little further away. An arm's length, or to the other side of the table.

Take a breath and notice what changes in your body. Does the feeling get louder, quieter, or just different?

If the distance feels like too much, move it back closer. There is no right distance, only the one that works for you right now.

Notice

How do you feel toward it?

Look at it again. Not: what does the part feel? But: how do you feel toward it?

Look again, now that the other part has stepped back a little.

Does this feeling have room for the part, or does it have an opinion about it?

Notice

That's another part

That makes sense.

And here's the interesting thing: that feeling probably belongs to another part. One that has an opinion about the first one. Parts often come in pairs like that, and it's completely normal.

Try this:

“Thank you. I see you too. Would you be willing to step back a little, just for a few minutes, so I can get to know this one?”

If it helps, give this second part an object too, and place it a little to the side.

It might step back, or it might not. Both are fine.

Notice

Not today, and that's okay

Sometimes a part isn't ready to step back. It usually has good reasons, and it doesn't need to be pushed.

And look at what you did: you noticed one part, and then you noticed a second part with an opinion about it. A few minutes ago, you might have just been both of them at once.

That noticing is the real skill. It's where all parts work begins.

There it is

That's a part of you

That curiosity isn't coming from the part. It's coming from you.

A little while ago, you were this feeling. Now you're sitting across from it, looking at it, and you can feel something toward it.

That's unblending. It's the first move in all parts work, and you just did it on your own.

If you like, let the part know you see it. You don't have to say anything clever. Something like:

“I see you. Thank you for trying so hard.”

Why we stop here

Enough for one evening

You might feel a pull to keep going. That's the curiosity working!

Here's why I'd stop here for now. Parts like this one usually have a job, and often that job is to protect something more tender underneath. Getting to know what it protects is good work. It goes best with someone holding the space with you, at a pace your whole system says yes to. Not alone at the kitchen table.

So for now: thank the part for showing up. Put the object back, or keep the drawing somewhere you'll see it.
